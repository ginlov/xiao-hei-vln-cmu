The rule

Both times we broke it, the data said so within one run

Let the VLM judge whether the scanner can be trusted same_space: true for all 52 sightings — including chairs on the far side of a glass door
Let the VLM estimate distance by perspective systematically 1.81× long; 21% within 1.5×

Both failures are the same shape. Glass, open structure and an occluder in front all surface as one contradiction — between the measured range and the size the object would have to be at that range. That contradiction is a multiplication we can do ourselves. Asking a model to notice it introduces judgment where arithmetic suffices.

Both were pre-registered proposals in TASK 26, and one of them was the option this project's own recommendation had ranked most feasible.

The loop

One model call per observation, and nothing else

VLM decides geometry decides

# repeat up to 6 times equirect, scan, terrain, pose ← capture() 1920×640 · /registered_scan /terrain_map · /state_estimation faces ← unwrap(equirect) 4 × 640² gnomonic, FOV 100° reply ← VLM(prompt(phrase), faces, previous = last_crop) if not reply.visible: θ ← yaw(pose) − radians(reply.explore.heading_deg) drive(pose.xy + 0.8 · [cos θ, sin θ]); continue box, face ← reply.box_2d, reply.image_index if reply.relation in {closest_to, farthest_from, between}: box, face ← resolve_relation(reply, scan, pose) § next slide wp ← next_waypoint(box, face, scan, pose) goal ← nearest point the converter will accept, toward the target if predicted_movement(goal) < 0.25 m: stop no drive, no call result ← drive(goal) if wp.committed and (result.gap ≤ 0.35 or result.moved < 0.25): arrived

The model is consulted once per observation and answers one question. Every branch, threshold and metre below that line is geometry.

The dominant phrasing

Asked which lantern was closest to the fan, it returned the fan

Comparative phrases — closest, nearest, farthest, between — are 47–60% of the official question set. Asked for the winner directly, the model answered the first live relational phrase with the anchor. So the prompt asks it to enumerate instead, and geometry compares.

candidates ← [lift(b) for b in reply.candidates] ≥ 2 needed anchors ← [lift(b) for b in reply.anchors] ≥ 1 needed if too few of either: fall back to the model's own pick the only answer available score(c) = Σ |c − a| over anchors if relation is "between" = |c − anchors[0]| otherwise return argmin score argmax for "farthest_from"

When not to believe a range · 1

The lidar is tilted forward, so it is blind behind and below

azimuth from dead ahead7°52° 82°112°142°172°
lowest elevation returned−32°−26° −16°−5°+4° +9°

Measured as the 0.5th percentile of return elevation per 15° azimuth bin across seven scenes, repeating to within 1–3°. This is the entire explanation for “no lidar return”: all four such cases point below the floor at their bearing. Four for four.

A blind bearing does not merely lose the lift — it can return a confident wrong one. The cone widens upward until it finds something, and what it finds is the wall above the object. On the first live run a target at −120° lifted to 4.70 m against a true ~2.9 m, with an implied height of 0.62 m that the size gate happily accepted. So a blind lift is never committed.

The cone is body-fixed, so driving one leg swings the bearing toward 0° where the floor is −32°. Observed live twice: −120° → +23° and −146° → +50°. The step is not a concession; it is what makes the next lift sound.

When not to believe a range · 2

One multiplication removes half the false positives

size_gate(range, h°): 0.15 m ≤ 2 · range · tan(h° / 2) ≤ 4.0 m
Gatehitsfalse positivescost
none72.2%24.1%—
A — decline when the cone is empty 72.2%16.7%zero
A + B — plus the size band 70.4% 11.1%1 hit

Gate A is free — no threshold, no model. Refusing to answer without depth data is strictly better than answering from none.

Replace the crude band with per-class size priors loses at every tolerance — 68.5% / 11.1% against 70.4% / 11.1%

Two structural reasons: 12 of 52 phrases have no prior and fall through to the band anyway, and every surviving false positive is a wrong instance whose size matches the right one. Kept behind a flag rather than deleted — the argument was sound, only the data refutes it.

The platform

The converter does not clamp. It discards.

/way_point_with_heading is not a position command. A waypoint inside the obstacle inflation is not moved along our approach until it is legal — it is thrown away, and a global re-minimisation picks a replacement that owes nothing to the direction we meant. Asking for a point 0.19 m from a lantern sent the robot 1.08 m to the far side of it.

travArea ← /terrain_map points with height-above-ground < 0.05 m obstacleArea ← the rest both voxel 0.05 legal(p) ← no obstacleArea point within 0.75 m of p snap(waypoint, vehicle): candidates ← travArea within 5.0 m of the vehicle return argmin over legal candidates of |p − waypoint| + 0.5 · |p − vehicle| settle(waypoint, vehicle): # the C++ re-snaps at 10 Hz and the vehicle v ← vehicle # term re-centres as the robot moves, so the while |snap(waypoint, v) − v| ≥ 0.3: # resting place is the fixed v ← v stepped toward snap(waypoint, v) # point, not the first snap return v

So instead of publishing range − standoff and discovering the result, publish the legal point nearest the target — a local minimum of the converter's own score, republished as itself.

The uncomfortable finding

The bar we set ourselves is not reachable through this interface

STANDOFF_M = 0.6 came from the median distance the reference trajectories keep from the objects they name. We treated it as a target we were falling short of. It is not reachable at all:

42% of the reference trajectory sits inside obstacleDisThre

Its closest approach has 0.36 m of clearance against a required 0.75. The converter would reject half of the organisers' own demonstration.

1.16 m nearest legal standing point, japanese_room lantern

Floor cells exist right up to it — 97 within 0.5 m of the box — but the best clearance any of them has is 0.53 m. Wall, lantern and potted plant inflate into each other and seal the corner.

Driving closer makes it worse. Re-measured after one leg with 3× the terrain observed, the nearest legal stand recedes from 1.16 m to 1.32 m: newly seen ground is mostly newly seen obstacle. Where the robot already stands beats anything a further waypoint achieves.

Whether “go near X” scores at the ~1 m the platform allows for objects in corners is unknown — the README publishes no distance threshold, only “whether it follows the path constraints”. Worth asking the organisers, because it is not a question our code can answer.

Compliance

The arrival signal we were using is not ours to use

The autonomy stack publishes /way_point_reached, and it is not among the five topics the README allows an AI module to read at test time. Both halves of the replacement come from /state_estimation.

gap ≤ 0.35 m we got where we asked — our own distance to our own waypoint
no motion > 0.05 m for 4 s the stack will not go closer

Validated against the very signal it replaces. Driving into the tea table, our pose-only number read 0.95631006 against the stack's 0.95631009. In the latest japanese_room run the disallowed topic never fired at all — our predicate got there first.

A distance test alone does hang, because the snap means we never reach our literal XY. The missing half was never a topic — it was noticing that the vehicle had stopped. app/main.py still subscribes to it in two places, and that is submission-blocking.

Held to the same standard as the last deck

What is measured, and what is load-bearing but not

Measured

  • 72.2% localisation from a single start-pose frame; 11.1% false positives after gates; 0.11 m median error, 54 pairs.
  • The blind cone, on seven scenes — and its prediction that turning fixes it, confirmed live twice.
  • The converter's behaviour, to ~0.1 m, on three drives.
  • Cost: 2 990 in / 460 out tokens = $0.0265 a call.

Not measured

  • Everything in the grounding result is one frame from the start pose. Distance, occlusion and out-of-vocabulary targets are untested.
  • The relational branch has one live phrase behind it, not a sweep — and relational phrasings are 47–60% of the official set.
  • Multi-constraint instructions (go near A, then take the path near B to C) are not implemented; the loop drives to one object and stops.
  • target_state and same_object_as_previous are logged and gate nothing.

The previous deck retracted six beliefs when the numbers came back, and said the proposal on its next slides would be held to the same standard. Three of the retractions on this one — blind lifts, per-class size priors, and the platform “clamp” — are its own.
